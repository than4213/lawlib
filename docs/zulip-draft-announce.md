**Topic: Lawlib: US tax & benefit law as a Lean library**

[To review together before posting. Key beats, dual-audience:]

1. What it is: a Lean 4 library containing the computable core of US
   tax-and-transfer law — 10,362 exact-rational, date-indexed
   parameters and ~3,200 total formula translations (EITC, CTC, SNAP,
   SSI, ACA premium credit diff-validated against PolicyEngine, the
   400k-line Python microsimulation model used by think tanks,
   journalists, and benefit-screening tools).
2. Everything is generated by a rejection-based transpiler (pe2lean,
   Apache-2.0); wrong translation is treated as worse than no
   translation; nightly differential CI runs 100k randomized households
   against executed PolicyEngine with zero tolerance beyond float32
   noise. Byte-exact re-extraction is CI-enforced.
3. The theorems: the 2023 IRS EIC table (10,120 cells parsed from the
   1040 instructions) is reproduced cell-for-cell by a reverse-
   engineered generator, kernel-checked via native_decide; EITC proven
   continuous; CTC's $50 benefit cliffs completely enumerated; and an
   independent Catala encoding of §32 proves the statute's literal
   arithmetic differs from administered practice by exactly 24¢/50¢.
4. Honest TCB note: table/atlas theorems use native_decide; symbolic
   theorems (continuity, closed forms) are plain kernel proofs; the
   claims layer (props, never asserted) keeps `#print axioms` clean and
   makes every reality-facing statement an explicit hypothesis.
5. What we want from the community: naming/idiom review, interest in
   the claims pattern, and the FromJson bug thread (already posted) as
   our toolchain contribution.
